Field research pilot

An early-stage field research project focused on apprenticeship and documentation in Northern Portugal.

Studium is an early-stage field research project exploring how small-scale apprenticeship and documentation projects might support craft and land-based knowledge transmission in Northern Portugal.

The first phase focuses on whether a small number of practitioners are willing to participate in a documented apprenticeship pilot under clear consent and review conditions.

Current pilot

One region, three practitioner relationships, one tested review process.

Location

Early conversations and site research are currently concentrated in Northern Portugal, with exploratory attention on the Douro and surrounding regions. The first long-term field site has not been selected yet.

Participants

Target: three practitioners or stewards who consent to documented conversations, workshop photography and a small pilot learning model.

Craft focus

Current research interests include vineyard work, dry stone construction, cork landscapes, ceramics and basketry traditions connected to the region.

Governance responsibility

Future field participation depends on local review and consent.

Local review

The pilot assumes future participation from local practitioners, associations, cooperatives or other review partners before any public apprenticeship activity begins.

Consent structures

The proposed consent process would define what can be documented, published, archived, restricted or removed before any material is shared publicly.

Student screening

Any future participation process would remain small and application-based during the pilot stage.

Cultural protections

Some practices may remain undocumented or private if practitioners or local communities do not want them recorded or shared.

Ecological safeguards

Any future field visits would need to respect seasonal work cycles, environmental conditions and the limits defined by local participants.

Dispute mediation

The proposed review model would allow local partners or participants to pause documentation activity if concerns emerge.

Read the operating model for steward authority

Documentation method

No expansion before documented fieldwork.

  • Interview process: record practitioner biography, lineage, teaching capacity, continuity risk and consent boundaries.
  • Field recording: photograph workshops and surrounding environments only after explicit permission, with clear attribution and documented consent boundaries.
  • Cultural permissions: sensitive practices require partner review before publication.
  • Archive standards: preserve source notes, image credits, license terms and removal rights.
  • Partner review: publish case studies only after practitioner and steward review.

Operating principles

Small scale first

Start with one region and a few consent-based practitioner relationships. Let field evidence set the pace of growth.

Limited participation

Any pilot participation process would remain small, application-based and dependent on practitioner approval.

Practitioner-led participation

Practitioners and stewards define what is appropriate to teach, document and share.

Documented consent

Documentation activities should begin only after expectations, permissions and review boundaries are discussed clearly.

Long-term accountability

The pilot should remain accountable to participating practitioners and local review partners as documentation develops.
