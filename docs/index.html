Studium

Pilot governance network

Place-based apprenticeship, documentation and cultural preservation.

Studium is currently a small-scale pilot framework for learning from craftspeople and land stewards through documented fieldwork, local review and carefully limited participation.

This begins as an early prototype for testing whether apprenticeship, cultural documentation and steward authority can work together in one region before any wider expansion.

Governance responsibility

Stewards carry operational authority.

Local review

Participation should be approved by the practitioner and, where relevant, a local steward, cooperative, elder or named partner organization.

Consent structures

Students, practitioners and stewards must agree on what can be documented, published, archived, restricted, or removed.

Student screening

Application-based access. Applicants should explain intent, relevant experience, time commitment and willingness to follow local rules.

Cultural protections

High-sensitivity knowledge requires additional review, limited media and the right for communities to decline participation.

Ecological safeguards

Field visits must respect seasonality, carrying capacity, safety and the condition of land-based systems.

Dispute mediation

Stewards can pause documentation, reject participation, mediate harm reports and define reciprocity obligations.

Read the operating model for steward authority

Documentation method

Fieldwork before scale.

  • Interview process: record practitioner biography, lineage, teaching capacity, continuity risk and consent boundaries.
  • Field recording: photograph workshop and environment after explicit permission; prioritize accuracy, context and restraint.
  • Cultural permissions: sensitive practices require partner review before publication.
  • Archive standards: preserve source notes, image credits, license terms and removal rights.
  • Partner review: publish case studies only after practitioner and steward review.

Operating principles

Small scale first

Start with one region and a few consent-based practitioner relationships. Let field evidence set the pace of growth.

Access by trust

Participation is earned through intent, preparation and respect for local boundaries.

Practitioner-led participation

Practitioners and stewards define what is appropriate to teach, document and share.

Reciprocal engagement

Prioritize consent, practitioner benefit, contextual documentation and pressure-free participation.

Long-term reciprocity

Economic, archival and reputational benefits must return to practitioners and communities.
