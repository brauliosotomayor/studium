Purpose

This document defines the near-term funding and compensation model for Studium. It replaces marketplace-scale assumptions with a pilot economics posture: fund governance, consent, archive work, practitioner compensation and careful field documentation.

No public revenue claim should imply active supply, students, partners or bookings until verified.

Economic principle

Studium should pay for the time and risk it asks others to carry. Funding should support practitioner continuity, documentation ethics and pilot learning before platform growth.

Recommended near-term path

Stage Months Structure Purpose
Setup 0-6 Founder-funded operations plus small restricted budget Governance, consent, advisor formation
Sponsored pilot 6-12 Fiscal sponsorship or partner-hosted grant Fieldwork, archive setup, practitioner compensation
Local association path 12-18 Portuguese nonprofit association exploration Local legitimacy and long-term governance
Operating pilot 18-24 Grants, donations and limited earned revenue if approved Continue fieldwork and test study visit model

Fiscal sponsorship

Fiscal sponsorship is the preferred first funding structure if an aligned sponsor can support:

  • charitable or educational funding;
  • international fieldwork expenses;
  • practitioner payments;
  • documentation and archive costs;
  • advisor honoraria;
  • transparent accounting;
  • mission-aligned reporting.

Sponsor selection should be reviewed for control over funds, fee percentage, reporting burden, international compliance and compatibility with Portuguese work.

Portuguese nonprofit association path

Explore an associacao sem fins lucrativos when the pilot has:

  • at least 3 committed governance participants or members;
  • local advisor or partner support;
  • basic bylaws with mission lock;
  • accounting and banking path;
  • clear member decision rights;
  • data, consent and archive policies;
  • sustainable administrative capacity.

Do not create an association only for appearance. It should have implemented governance and local operating need.

Grant strategy

Priority grant categories:

Category Good fit Risk
Cultural heritage documentation Consent-based interviews, archive preparation Pressure to publish too much
Craft education Practitioner-led learning and transmission Student volume expectations
Rural development Local partner support and place-based learning Tourism framing
Community archive Metadata, access tiers, restricted archive work Tool-first implementation
Environmental stewardship Land-based practices and seasonal knowledge Overextending beyond craft pilot

Grant applications should include conservative outputs: relationships, consent workflows, archive records and evaluation, not scale.

Compensation model

Payee Compensation basis Notes
Practitioner Day rate, review fee, materials reimbursement Pay even if material remains restricted
Community or local partner Coordination fee or partner grant share Use written scope
Advisor Honorarium or paid review Avoid unpaid governance labor where possible
Local coordinator Hourly or monthly retainer Especially for language and logistics
Documentarian Contract fee Must follow consent workflow
Student participant Usually pays later, but may receive stipend in research setting Do not recruit until policy exists

Revenue models to avoid now

Model Why to avoid
Marketplace take rate Incentivizes booking volume before trust and safety
Instant booking Removes practitioner and steward control
Subscription access to archive Risks paywalling or exposing restricted knowledge
Sponsored content Can distort representation and practitioner consent
Influencer trips Conflicts with documentation ethics
Ranking or featured placement fees Incentivizes popularity over stewardship
AI training or dataset licensing Violates archive trust unless explicitly governed, and should be prohibited now

Limited earned revenue that may be tested later

Only after governance and agreements are in place:

  • paid study visits with strict capacity limits;
  • institution-sponsored field seminars;
  • grant-funded documentation workshops;
  • commissioned practitioner documentation returned to the practitioner;
  • public talks about the pilot method without revealing restricted materials.

Each must pass consent, compensation, safety and public language review.

Budget categories

The pilot budget should track:

  • legal and governance review;
  • advisor honoraria;
  • practitioner compensation;
  • local coordination;
  • translation and interpretation;
  • travel and lodging;
  • documentation equipment and services;
  • archive storage and access tooling;
  • insurance and safety preparation;
  • accounting and administration.

Funding acceptance checklist

Accept funding only if:

  • deliverables match consent and capacity;
  • no public claim exceeds verified relationships;
  • reporting does not require exposing restricted material;
  • practitioner compensation is included;
  • governance review is documented;
  • withdrawal and archive restrictions remain intact.

Studium does not currently operate an apprenticeship program, steward network or institutional partnership structure.
