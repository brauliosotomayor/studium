Purpose

This document defines the governance structure Studium needs before pilot fieldwork expands beyond founder-led research. It converts the Phase II stewardship principles into legal options, decision rights, founder authority limits, conflict resolution paths and sequencing.

Studium should not present itself as a governed institution until the legal entity, advisors and consent process are actually in place.

Legal structure options

Option Use case Advantages Constraints Recommended stage
Fiscal sponsorship Early grants, donations, research funding Faster setup; administrative support; credibility for funders Sponsor policies may constrain payments and international work Months 0-12
Portuguese nonprofit association Local governance and Portuguese pilot operations Local legitimacy; member governance; direct regional partnerships Requires bylaws, officers, accounting and compliance Months 6-18
U.S. nonprofit U.S. donor and institutional funding Familiar to U.S. funders Less local fit for Portugal pilot; setup burden Defer unless funding requires it
For-profit company Future booking or marketplace operations Flexible commercial contracts Underdeveloped mission lock; higher extraction risk Do not form before governance and consent model
Hybrid structure Nonprofit archive plus commercial services arm Separates mission work from revenue Complex; requires mature governance Future option only

Phase III recommendation: start with fiscal sponsorship while drafting a Portuguese nonprofit association path. Do not create a for-profit marketplace entity before consent, stewardship and compensation rules are tested.

Mission-lock logic

Studium's mission lock should protect three commitments:

  1. Fieldwork, documentation and apprenticeship serve practitioner and community continuity.
  2. Archive access follows consent and stewardship review, not audience demand.
  3. Technology supports governance, consent and documentation before scale.

The mission lock should appear in bylaws, sponsor agreements, advisor charters and practitioner agreements.

Advisory limits

Advisors provide review and constraint. They do not become proof of practitioner participation, partner endorsement or community consent unless their role is explicitly documented.

Founder authority limits

The founder may:

  • maintain the repository and internal planning documents;
  • conduct desk research;
  • draft policies for review;
  • initiate conversations with advisors and potential partners;
  • maintain unpublished candidate records.

The founder may not unilaterally:

  • publish practitioner profiles without consent;
  • imply advisors, stewards or partners are committed before they are verified;
  • publish exact private locations without permission;
  • change consent or archive access terms after documentation is collected;
  • open student recruitment before compensation, liability and review policies exist;
  • expand the atlas publicly beyond the Northern Portugal pilot.

Conflict resolution

Conflict First response Escalation Final rule
Practitioner disputes public representation Take material offline within 5 business days Advisor + practitioner review Practitioner-approved wording controls
Community or steward objects to documentation Restrict access immediately Stewardship review meeting Restricted access remains default
Student violates media or fieldwork rules Suspend participation Advisor review if high-risk Removal and takedown if harm occurred
Founder and advisor disagree on publication Hold publication Advisory board review No publication without documented approval
Funding creates mission conflict Pause acceptance Governance advisor review Decline if consent or stewardship is compromised

Governance sequence

Phase Months Required outputs Gate
0. Internal preparation 0-2 Governance blueprint, consent workflow, archive schema No field documentation published
1. Advisor formation 2-4 Advisor candidates, role letters, first review meeting No public advisory claims until accepted
2. Legal path selection 3-6 Fiscal sponsor review or association setup memo No paid public programming
3. Pilot governance test 6-12 3 documented practitioner relationships under consent No expansion beyond pilot region
4. Association readiness 12-18 Draft bylaws, member roles, accounting path No app marketplace launch
5. Operating governance 18-24 Annual review, risk register review, archive audit Continue / pivot / pause decision

Immediate implementation priorities

  1. Identify 6-10 advisor candidates across the minimum board seats.
  2. Draft one-page advisor role letter with time expectations and authority limits.
  3. Compare fiscal sponsor and Portuguese association requirements.
  4. Create a written rule that governance, consent and archive controls precede pilot fieldwork.
  5. Record all governance decisions in a dated decision log before outreach claims become public.

Studium does not currently operate an apprenticeship program, steward network or institutional partnership structure.
