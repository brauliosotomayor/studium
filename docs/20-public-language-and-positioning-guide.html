Purpose

This guide defines what Studium may say publicly, what remains internal and which claims are forbidden at the current stage.

Public language should match evidence: pre-fieldwork, Northern Portugal pilot, governance and consent in preparation, practitioner relationships not yet publicly verified.

Current public position

Studium may be described as:

An early-stage pilot for place-based apprenticeship, documentation and cultural preservation in Northern Portugal.

Expanded version:

Studium is preparing a small Northern Portugal pilot to test whether craftspeople, land stewards and high-engagement learners can participate in consent-based field documentation and apprenticeship structures.

What may be public

Topic Allowed language

What remains internal

  • speculative global atlas and corridor strategy;
  • Founding 13 planning language;
  • unverified practitioner names or candidates;
  • advisor candidates before acceptance;
  • partner target lists before agreement;
  • sensitive archive material;
  • exact private locations;
  • internal risk register details;
  • financial models implying scale.

Forbidden public claims

Do not say or imply:

  • Studium has active practitioners, stewards or partners unless verified;
  • Studium represents a region, lineage, culture or named practitioner group;
  • global corridors are operational;
  • the atlas is authoritative;
  • student applications are open before policies exist;
  • an app, marketplace or booking system is available;
  • practitioners can be discovered, ranked or booked;
  • sensitive, Indigenous or community-held knowledge is available to access;
  • advisors or institutions endorse Studium before written confirmation.

Language to avoid

Avoid public language that is:

  • mythic or civilizational;
  • sacred, zodiac or planetary;
  • destiny or legacy-scale;
  • "global master network";
  • "world's living masters";
  • "Airbnb for apprenticeships";
  • "marketplace for ancient knowledge";
  • "unlock hidden traditions";
  • "discover secret places";
  • "top experiences" or ranking language.

Use practical language: pilot, fieldwork, documentation, consent, practitioner review, local review, archive, compensation and governance.

Homepage constraints

The current homepage may include:

  • pilot status;
  • Northern Portugal scope;
  • what exists today;
  • what remains experimental;
  • governance and consent commitments;
  • links to internal working documents when appropriate.

It should not include:

  • named practitioner profiles without consent;
  • active booking or application calls to action;
  • broad atlas claims;
  • partner logos unless agreements exist;
  • claims of institutional authority;
  • exact locations for unverified or private sites.

Approved copy blocks

Short

Studium is an early-stage pilot for place-based apprenticeship, documentation and cultural preservation in Northern Portugal.

Method

The current work is governance, consent design, archive planning and careful practitioner outreach. Public documentation will follow only after consent and review.

Product status

Studium is not building an app or marketplace before governance, consent and archive controls are tested.

Fieldwork status

Practitioner relationships are pending field validation and should not be described as active unless documented approval exists.

Review checklist

Before publishing any public copy, confirm:

  1. Does every claim match current evidence?
  2. Are all names, images and locations approved?
  3. Does the copy avoid scale, ranking and tourism framing?
  4. Does it distinguish internal research from field-verified relationships?
  5. Would a practitioner or local partner recognize the claim as accurate?
  6. Is governance, consent or archive readiness overstated?

If uncertain, keep the claim internal.

Studium does not currently operate an apprenticeship program, steward network or institutional partnership structure.
