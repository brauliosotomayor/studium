Pilot Project: Paços de Gaiolo Living Landscape Lab
(Small, Measurable, Scalable)
Step 1: Define the Scope (Keep It Contained)
Do NOT start with the whole property.
Start with:
•	500–2,000 m² max
•	One clearly defined zone (slope, terrace, orchard edge, degraded patch)
You want:
•	Clear boundaries
•	Clear before/after documentation
•	Clear learning loop
This is a lab, not a manifesto.
⸻
Step 2: Baseline Study (Month 1–2)
Before planting anything, study.
A. Site Analysis
•	Slope & drainage pattern
•	Soil type (clay? schist? sandy loam?)
•	Existing vegetation (native vs invasive)
•	Sun exposure zones
•	Wind corridors
B. Soil Test
Send soil for:
•	Organic matter %
•	pH
•	Nutrient profile
Portugal’s north (Douro region) often has:
•	Shallow soils
•	Erosion vulnerability
•	Acidic tendencies
Don’t assume. Test.
⸻
Step 3: Native Ecosystem Research
You need to understand what “native” means specifically to Paços de Gaiolo.
Research:
•	Mediterranean oak systems (Quercus suber, Quercus faginea)
•	Native shrubs (Cistus, Arbutus unedo, Lavandula stoechas)
•	Fire-adapted species
•	Pollinator networks
•	Riparian plants if near water
Contact:
•	Universidade de Trás-os-Montes e Alto Douro (UTAD)
•	Local forestry engineers
•	Portuguese native plant nurseries
You are apprenticing here.
⸻
Step 4: Define 3 Clear Goals
Keep them measurable.
Example:
1.	Increase native species diversity from X to Y within 24 months.
2.	Reduce surface runoff/erosion on slope by X%.
3.	Increase pollinator presence (tracked via observation logs).
No vague language.
Numbers.
⸻
Step 5: Design the Intervention (Simple, Not Sexy)
A. Remove Invasives
Acacia and eucalyptus are common issues in Portugal.
Remove carefully and strategically.
Do not clear everything at once.
B. Build Water Logic First
Before planting:
•	Contour swales
•	Mulch basins
•	Stone check dams (traditional Portuguese technique)
•	Soil carbon building (compost, biochar)
Water > plants.
C. Plant in Guilds
Do NOT plant ornamental rows.
Plant clusters that mimic succession:
•	Canopy species
•	Understory shrubs
•	Nitrogen fixers
•	Ground cover
•	Pollinator attractors
Nature is layered.
Copy that.
